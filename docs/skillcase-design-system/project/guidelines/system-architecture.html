SkillCase design system — consolidation

Phase 4. No screens were built and no product surface was audited. This report answers one question from the evidence already gathered: what is the actual design system of the SkillCase product? Where the answer is "we do not know", it says so.

Date 27 Aug 2026Method Code as behavioural truth; Figma as visual referenceScope System definition onlyNothing refactored in production

1Executive summary

SkillCase has a real design system, and it is smaller than the component count suggests. Ninety-three exports resolve to roughly a dozen ideas that recur: a navy hub chrome, a gold primary action, a card-on-wash surface model, a lettered choice row, a status panel, a scored result, and a mascot who speaks. Everything else is those ideas dressed for a product area.

Three findings shape the phase.

1. The 838 tokens are 633 tokens. The figure everyone has been quoting — including earlier reports in this project — is a declaration count. 205 of those declarations are dark-theme redeclarations of names already defined in light. Nothing is wrong; the number was just being read as inventory when it is inventory plus one theme. Corrected throughout below.

2. Three token layers coexist and none references another. --colors-* / --component-colors-* (484 light + 205 dark, imported from Untitled UI), --sc-* (102, SkillCase brand), --app-* (47, app geometry and motion). The SkillCase brand values are not wired into the kit's semantic layer — --component-colors-* still points at Untitled UI greys and indigos. Every SkillCase component therefore styles from hex literals or --sc-* directly, bypassing the semantic tier entirely. This is the single largest structural gap in the system.

3. The system is ready for some of the product and not for the rest, and the boundary is exactly the audit line. Guided German, the job pipeline, the payment funnel, the home hub and the B1 exam system can be built from this system today. Profile, content, scholarship and most of A1/A2 cannot, because nobody has read them. The readiness answer is therefore not one word (§12).

Three of fifteen states are global. That ratio is the finding, not a shortfall. Loading, empty and error are genuinely the same everywhere because they carry no product meaning. Correct, locked and completed carry a great deal of product meaning, and the product has deliberately said different things with them. A system that flattened those would be less accurate than the product it describes.

A well-evidenced, honestly-scoped system with a thin upper layer. The three largest gaps, in order: accessibility is undefined (2/10 — and no amount of provenance compensates for it), templates barely exist (3/10, so every new screen is still assembled by hand), and the semantic token tier is unwired (DS-1, which is what keeps components on hex literals and makes consistency a matter of discipline rather than structure).

The score is deliberately not averaged upward by the two 8s. Documentation and engineering alignment are the dimensions that make a system trustworthy; they are not the dimensions that make it productive.

12Readiness

ALMOST — and the boundary is the audit line

Yes, for audited territory. A new screen in Guided German, the job pipeline, the payment funnel, the home hub or the B1 exam system can be built from this system now. The components exist, they trace to production, their states are known and their divergences are documented. Someone unfamiliar with the product would produce something recognisably SkillCase.

No, for the rest. Profile, content, scholarship and the A1/A2 content screens have not been read. Building them from this system would mean inventing product behaviour, which is precisely what the operating model forbids.

Two gaps make even the "yes" conditional. Accessibility is undefined, so any screen produced now inherits an unspecified accessibility posture. And with one template, screen production is still assembly rather than instantiation — which means consistency depends on whoever is assembling.

13Recommended next phase

One step only. Not executed.

Wire the semantic token tier. Author the missing layer that maps SkillCase brand values onto named roles — --sc-action-primary, --sc-surface-card, --sc-text-secondary, --sc-border-subtle and so on — sourced from --sc-* and consumed by every component in place of hex literals.

It is the highest-leverage move because it is the only one that improves four dimensions at once: tokens (DS-1), cross-product consistency (a colour becomes a decision made once), components (DS-2 disappears), and dark theme (205 dark declarations currently have nothing SkillCase-side to override). It is bounded — one stylesheet and a mechanical pass over ~60 components — and it is not blocked on any product decision, unlike the pass mark or the pause question.

The two obvious alternatives are weaker right now. Accessibility scores lowest but is a specification exercise that will need to be applied to components anyway — cheaper after the token pass, not before. Templates would raise productivity most, but building templates on hex literals bakes the debt into the thing everything else is generated from.
